International Treaty Limits Orbital Space Debris in High-Altitude Zones

By Sarah Jenkins | Published July 30, 2026

GENEVA — Delegates at the UN Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space unanimously voted to enforce strict de-orbiting deadlines for all newly launched commercial satellites.

Under the new treaty provisions, satellite operators must ensure spacecraft actively de-orbit within five years of mission completion using propulsion or electrodynamic tethers.

The framework aims to prevent cascade collisions in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) as satellite constellations continue to expand.
