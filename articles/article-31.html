Gene-Editing Therapy Receives Full Approval for Sickle Cell Disease

By Dr. Aris Thorne | Published July 30, 2026

WASHINGTON — Health regulators granted full non-conditional approval today for a single-dose CRISPR gene-editing therapy that permanently cures sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia.

By modifying a patient's own hematopoietic stem cells to reactivate fetal hemoglobin production, the therapy eliminates painful vaso-occlusive crises and hospitalizations.

Patient advocacy groups praised the milestone while calling for global access initiatives to make the cure available in low-resource settings.
